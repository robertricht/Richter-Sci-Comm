I deliver talks on science that invite audiences to look at the world differently. From classical mechanics to quantum technology, from pattern formation in animals to spiral growth in plants, my talks explore how deep scientific ideas become visible in nature, technology, and everyday experience.

I am especially interested in topics that reveal the beauty and power of scientific thinking: the mathematics behind stripes, spots, and phyllotactic spirals, the physics of motion and instability, the emergence of order from simple rules, and the fascinating promises and limits of modern quantum technologies.

Whether for public audiences, schools, universities, festivals, or interdisciplinary events, I aim to make science tangible: clear in explanation, rich in examples, and open to curiosity.
