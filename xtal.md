<header><h1>xtal — Modular Light Sculptures</h1></header>
<p class="xtal-lead">Create your own light sculpture. Shape it to your space.</p>
<p>Flat LED modules become three-dimensional lighting that you assemble, reshape and extend. Developed by Robert Richter and Tobias Schubert, xtal combines light, structure and electrical contact in the circuit boards themselves.</p>
<figure class="xtal-photo xtal-hero"><a href="{{ '/assets/images/xtal-room.jpg' | relative_url }}" target="_blank" rel="noopener"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/xtal-room.jpg' | relative_url }}" alt="Illuminated xtal pendant prototype above a dining table" loading="lazy" decoding="async" /></a><figcaption>A working pendant prototype in a living space.</figcaption></figure>
<div class="xtal-section"><h2>One system. Different spatial forms.</h2>
<p>From a compact table sculpture to a suspended composition: the modules connect directly, without separate connectors. Users determine the final form and can later add, rearrange or replace individual elements.</p>

<div class="xtal-section"><h2>Flat modules. Spatial light.</h2>
<p>The same flat parts that form the sculpture can be packed densely before assembly. Established PCB fabrication and automated component placement offer a route to scalable production; customers assemble the final lamp to suit their space.</p>

<div class="xtal-section"><h2>From working prototype to first product</h2>
<p>A working prototype already provides room illumination. The system is under development and is not yet a commercially released product. Our next steps are to test connection reliability and manufacturing tolerances, develop protected power input and define safe operating limits.</p>
<p>Alongside technical testing, we will validate whether first-time users can assemble a kit independently, what customers are willing to pay and whether each kit can deliver a positive contribution margin.</p>
