Nature Structure explores how complex, organic-looking forms can emerge from simple rules, repetition, and feedback. Across three interactive exhibits, visitors encounter pattern formation at the intersection of mathematics, physics, and biology: flower-like curves generated by mechanical constraints, phyllotactic animations inspired by plant growth, and fractal-like image structures produced through video feedback. 

Rather than presenting nature as something mysterious or decorative, the exhibit shows how rich structures can arise from straightforward ordering principles. Small changes accumulate into spirals, blossoms, and self-similar patterns that recall succulents, shells, Romanesco, or animal markings. Bloom is based on the animation method invented by John Edmark, whose rotating sculptures use the golden angle and synchronized stroboscopic viewing to make phyllotactic structures appear to grow and unfold. 

Nature Structure invites visitors to experiment directly: adjust parameters, observe how patterns shift, and develop an intuitive sense for periodicity, growth, self-organization, and emergence—where complexity is not imposed, but generated through iteration.
