Cooking with liquid nitrogen is a high-impact live performance that makes thermodynamics tangible through dramatic, hands-on demonstrations. Using liquid nitrogen as an extreme cooling agent, we explore rapid cooling, phase changes, and how materials behave when temperature drops within seconds—often accompanied by rolling fog, surprising sounds, and memorable visual effects.

### Signature demos (and what audiences learn)
- **Self-inflating balloon:** liquid nitrogen boils off and expands into gas, inflating a balloon.  
  *Phase change, gas expansion, and why “a little liquid can become a lot of gas.”*

- **Hammering a nail with a banana:** chilled objects become unexpectedly rigid and strong.  
  *Material properties vs. temperature, brittleness vs. toughness.*

- **Roses (and other objects) shatter like glass:** frozen water and plant structures turn brittle.  
  *Thermal shock, brittleness, and why some materials “switch behavior” when cooled.*

- **Rubber vs. cryo:** a rubber ball becomes hard and can crack or bounce like glass.  
  *Glass transition and viscoelasticity—how polymers change state.*

- **Ball-and-ring (shrink fit):** cooling a metal part makes it contract and fit through a ring again.  
  *Thermal contraction and precision effects of temperature.*

- **Fog vortex rings:** the cold nitrogen fog becomes a medium for visible airflow—rings, swirls, and vortices.  
  *Fluid dynamics you can literally see.*

- **The “screaming” ping-pong ball (or whistle effect):** rapid boiling drives airflow and sound.  
  *Pressure, flow, and how phase change can produce motion and sound.*

- **Magnetic effects: slow magnet in a copper tube:** a magnet falls noticeably slower through copper due to eddy currents.  
  *Electromagnetic induction, eddy currents, and magnetic braking (Lenz’s law).*

### Interactive elements (audience-friendly)
Depending on the venue and format, the show includes small participation moments that help audiences build intuition:
- **Warm vs. cold cup comparison:** what you *see* vs. what you *feel*—and why perception can be misleading.  
- **Material guessing game:** “What breaks? what bends? what rings?” (rubber, plastic, metal, wood).  
- **Seat-level mini demos (no LN₂ in the audience):** prepared objects are pre-cooled backstage and briefly demonstrated safely.

### Stagecraft that makes it pop
Liquid nitrogen performances are naturally theatrical—and we lean into that:
- **Fog as a light tool:** camera/phone lights, spotlights, and lasers become visible in the mist, creating striking silhouettes.  
- **Sound design:** cracks, squeaks, whistles, and metallic “tings” become part of the storytelling—announced and framed so the audience understands what they’re hearing.

*Note: edible elements (e.g., instant ice cream) are optional and venue-/safety-dependent. The show can be delivered as a pure demonstration format if needed.*
